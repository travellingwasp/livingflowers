Short intro

North-facing windows typically provide lower light levels than east/south/west exposures. This short guide helps renters pick plants and decide where to place them. Use the quick checklist first, then try the light estimator if you need a finer read: https://windowplantlab.com/light-meter/

Quick checklist — use this first (30–60 seconds)

- Step 1 — Observe: stand at the window for a few minutes. Does direct sun ever fall on the spot? (If yes, treat like a brighter exposure.)
- Step 2 — Measure roughly: note whether the spot is within 0.5 m (close), 0.5–1.5 m (near), or >1.5 m (far) from the glass; farther distances mean less usable light.
- Step 3 — Look for obstructions: heavy curtains, external overhangs, adjacent buildings, or trees reduce light further — mark the spot as 'obstructed' if present.
- Step 4 — Choose tolerant plants (see table below) and place them closer to the window if you can (closer = better in north-facing exposures).
- Step 5 — Monitor for signs of low light after 2–4 weeks: slow new growth, leaf yellowing, or leggy stems suggest the spot is too dim — move closer or try supplemental light.

Recommended plants for north-facing windows (compact reference)

| Plant (common name) | Why it works | Placement tip |
|---|---|---|
| Snake plant (Sansevieria / Dracaena trifasciata) | Very tolerant of low light and inconsistent watering | Place within 0.5–1.0 m of window; tolerates corners |
| Pothos (Epipremnum aureum) | Adapts to low light; trailing habit makes it flexible | Keep near the window and rotate periodically to avoid lopsided growth |
| ZZ plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) | Stores water in rhizomes; slow-growing in low light | Best within 1.0 m of the window; do not overwater |
| Peace lily (Spathiphyllum) | Tolerates lower light and signals stress (drooping) clearly | Place closer to the window and water when soil feels dry at 2 cm depth |
| Cast-iron plant (Aspidistra elatior) | Very tolerant of dim indoor spots and neglect | Good for far-from-window corners within 1.5 m if there is reflected light |

How to use this guide

- If you want a slightly more precise result, try the Plant Light Estimator to combine direction, distance, and obstructions into an estimated light rating: https://windowplantlab.com/light-meter/
- If the plant shows poor growth after a month, move it closer, rotate it, or consider a small LED grow lamp for 2–3 hours of supplemental light per day.
